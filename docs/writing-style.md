# How we write

Applies to every visible word on the site, German and US. Write like someone who has compared a lot of sauna listings and is helping a friend choose one.

## What we do

- Say the useful thing first. Numbers, sizes, prices and the catch.
- One idea per paragraph. Two to four sentences. Mixed sentence length.
- Plain headings that name the topic ("Passt sie ins Bad?", "What it costs to run"). A heading is not a conclusion and not a slogan.
- Talk to the reader ("du", "you"). Use "wir"/"we" only for what we actually do.
- Say plainly what we do not know: "Der Hersteller nennt keinen Wandabstand." / "JNH doesn't list a clearance."
- Say it once. If the table shows it, the text does not repeat it.
- Leave a section out when there is nothing to say.

## What we do not do

- No invented tests, experience, customers, authors or anecdotes. Nobody here has sat in these saunas.
- No talk about our own database. The reader does not care about records.
- No headings above headings (kickers, eyebrows), no numbered decoration (01, 02, 03), no arrows in text.
- No defensive disclaimers after every fact. One note per page at most.
- No paired slogans, no "nicht X, sondern Y" / "not X but Y" as a reflex, no three-part lists out of habit.
- No em dashes as a style device, no colon-heavy sentences.

## Words to avoid

German: Datensatz, belegt, dokumentiert (for our own data), verifiziert, nachvollziehbar, Einordnung, einordnen, ausgewiesen, rechnerisch, Produktfläche (say Stellfläche), Planungsfläche, Raumlogik, belastbar, maßgeblich, Kandidat, Auswahl endet bei, Momentaufnahme, Logik, Profil, Grenze dieser Auswahl, bleibt sichtbar, wird getrennt geführt.

US: documented, record, recorded, configuration (say model or version), source-based, reviewed, evidence, envelope, attached to, remains open, planning starting point, scope, boundary, entry, establish, normalize, carries, surfaces, underscore.
